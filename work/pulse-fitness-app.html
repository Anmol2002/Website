01Overview

Workouts, meals, progress and a training squad, organised around one question: what do I do today?

Pulse is a fitness app that turns a user's goals into a weekly plan, then helps them stick to it with nutrition tracking, progress charts and a small training squad. I designed the onboarding, the core training and nutrition flows, the tracking screens and a design system that keeps 39 screens consistent.

39screens designed
3-steppersonalised setup
5core areas: train, eat, track, squad, profile
1component system

02The challenge

What had to be solved

Most fitness apps open on a feed of content and leave people to work out what to do. Beginners give up because there is no clear next step, and regular users get lost between workouts, meal logs and stats spread across different tabs.

Design goals

  1. Make the first session happen on day one
  2. Put today's workout and calories on the home screen
  3. Keep logging fast enough to do between sets
  4. Use community for motivation without turning it into a social feed

03Process

How I approached it

  1. Start with the goalA three-step setup (goal, favourite workouts, activity level) creates a plan before the user sees the home screen.
  2. Design the daily loopHome answers what to do today: activity ring, today's workout and a one-tap start.
  3. Connect the planPlans, a weekly schedule and live workouts share the same cards, so the week and the session always match.
  4. Track what mattersNutrition, progress, sleep and habits each get one focused screen instead of one crowded stats page.

04Onboarding and sign-up

Sell the outcome, then get out of the way.

Three short onboarding screens show personalised training, smart nutrition and the squad. Login and sign-up are single screens with clear password rules.

05Personalised setup

Three questions, one plan.

Goal, preferred workouts and activity level are each one screen with a progress bar. The answers shape the first plan and the home screen the user lands on.

06Workouts and plans

From browsing to training in two taps.

Explore, workout details, popular programmes and plans share one card style. A plan overview shows progress by week, and the weekly schedule turns it into a calendar.

07Live workouts and tracking

Logging that fits between sets.

The live workout screen keeps the timer, current exercise and next set visible. Nutrition, progress and sleep each focus on one number with context, not a wall of charts.

08Squad, profile and upgrade

Motivation from a small group, not a feed.

Fitness squads are small groups with a shared goal and chat. Pulse Pro, profile and empty states reuse the same components.

09Design system

One system for every screen.

Colours, type, buttons, chips, cards and inputs were defined first and used everywhere, which is what kept 39 screens consistent.

10Decisions

Key design decisions

Plan before home screen

Users answer three questions before seeing the app, so the first screen they see already has a workout for them instead of an empty dashboard.

One hero number per screen

Nutrition shows calories left, sleep shows hours, progress shows the trend. Detail is one tap away, not stacked on top.

Squads over a public feed

Small groups keep motivation personal and avoid the comparison that pushes beginners away.

11Design system

Visual language

A vivid violet primary for actions and progress, soft neutral surfaces and photography-led cards for workouts. Chips, progress rings and stat tiles are shared components.

Violet#5B4CF0
Ink#14122B
Lilac#EEECFF
Mint#22C55E

TypeInter, bold numerals for stats

12Outcome

What was delivered

Pulse covers the full journey from first launch to a habit: setup, a plan, daily workouts, nutrition, tracking and a squad. Every screen answers one question, and the shared system makes new features quick to add.

If the project continues, next I would:

  • Add wearable sync for live heart rate
  • Test the setup flow with complete beginners
  • Design a coach view for trainers
