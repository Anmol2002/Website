01Overview

A right-to-left, single-product page that sells a premium knife set to UAE shoppers with trust, video and cash on delivery.

Luxury Kitchen sells a premium hand-forged knife set to home cooks across the UAE. I designed the Arabic, right-to-left landing page: a full buy box with cash on delivery, a demo video, a problem-and-solution section, the three knives explained, WhatsApp-style customer reviews, lifestyle photography, a bundle offer, product features and a closing call to action.

10sections on one page
RTLfull right-to-left layout
3knives explained one by one
1bundle upsell

02The challenge

What had to be solved

Single-product stores in the Gulf compete on trust. Shoppers want to see the product working, hear from real buyers and pay on delivery, and the whole page has to read naturally from right to left in Arabic.

Design goals

  1. Make the buy box complete on the first screen
  2. Prove quality with video and close-up photography
  3. Use reviews that feel real to local shoppers
  4. Lay out every section correctly right to left

03Process

How I approached it

  1. Buy box firstGallery, price with discount, stock note, delivery promise and a big green order button above the fold.
  2. Show it workingA demo video and a cutting sequence answer "does it really cut like that?"
  3. Local proofReviews shown as WhatsApp conversations and photos from customers in the UAE.
  4. Upsell and closeA cookware bundle, a features grid with video and a final dark call to action.

04Product and buy box

Everything needed to order on the first screen.

A large gallery sits on the left and, reading right to left, the title, rating, discounted price, stock and shipping notes, a green cash-on-delivery button, five trust icons and expandable product details.

05Demo video

Watch the knife do the work.

A video block shows the knives cutting meat and vegetables, with short claims about carbon steel, balance and a professional finish.

06Problem

Name the frustration first.

A warm gold section asks how often a blunt knife has ruined a meal, then shows the hammered blade slicing tomato in two tilted photos.

07The three knives

One knife for every kitchen job.

Three dark product cards explain the cleaver, the chopper and the boning knife, each with its own buy button.

08Customer reviews

Reviews that look like real conversations.

A carousel mixes WhatsApp chat screenshots and a customer photo with a star rating, the kind of proof Gulf shoppers trust most.

09Lifestyle

Made for how people cook in the UAE.

Three photos show everyday home cooking, barbecue gatherings and outdoor desert cooking, each with a short caption.

10Bundle offer

Complete the kitchen with one more click.

A gold section offers the matching pressure cooker as a bundle, with its own call to action and delivery note.

11Features

Built for daily use.

A product video sits beside four feature tiles: sharpness, grip, balance and durability, with a final order button.

12Final call to action

End on the blade.

A dark full-width hero with the hammered blade close up repeats the offer, followed by a footer with links, newsletter and trust badges.

13Decisions

Key design decisions

Mirror the whole layout

Gallery, arrows, carousels and icons all flip for right to left, not just the text alignment.

Green for the order button only

One green cash-on-delivery button stands out against the gold and brown palette everywhere it appears.

WhatsApp-style reviews

Chat screenshots feel more believable to UAE shoppers than polished testimonial cards.

14Design system

Visual language

Warm gold and deep brown with cream backgrounds, dark product cards, an Arabic display face for headlines and rounded gold pill buttons.

Gold#B8862F
Espresso#2A211B
Cream#F7F1EA
Order green#2DBE60

TypeArabic display + Arabic sans

15Outcome

What was delivered

Luxury Kitchen gets an Arabic product page that reads naturally right to left and builds trust in the order Gulf shoppers expect: see it, watch it, hear from buyers, then order with cash on delivery.

If the project continues, next I would:

  • Design an English version that shares the same layout
  • Add a one-page checkout instead of the order form
  • Test the bundle placement against a post-purchase upsell
