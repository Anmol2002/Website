01Overview

A bold, friendly store homepage that makes ordering custom branded merch feel simple, even for one item.

Nerds is a custom printing studio in Luxembourg that puts company logos on sustainable clothing, drinkware, office supplies and bags. I designed the store homepage: a product-collage hero, the brand promises, client logos, a three-step ordering process, illustrated categories, a best-seller shelf, an about block and a section for customers in Portuguese, Luxembourgish and German.

02The challenge

What had to be solved

Custom merch usually feels like a B2B quote form: minimum orders, unclear prices and long back-and-forth. Nerds wanted the opposite, a store that feels as easy as buying a T-shirt online while still winning company clients.

Design goals

  1. Say "no minimum order" clearly and early
  2. Show real products and prices, not just a quote form
  3. Prove that known brands already trust Nerds
  4. Give the brand a playful personality without losing clarity

03Process

How I approached it

  1. Product-led heroA collage of real merch in rounded capsules, with Shop now and Get in touch side by side.
  2. Promises up frontA yellow block with the satisfaction guarantee and trusted-brands proof right after the hero.
  3. Three simple stepsChoose your product, upload your logo, get it fast, each in one short line.
  4. Shop and storyCategory illustrations, a best-seller shelf with prices and colour swatches, then the about and language sections.

04Hero

Show the merch, not a stock photo.

A capsule collage of tees, hoodies, caps, mugs and bags sits next to the headline "Merch that matters", with clear Shop now and Get in touch buttons and a Google rating in the top bar.

05Promises

Answer the worries before they are asked.

A scrolling strip repeats the three promises (easy customisation, no complex setups, clear pricing), then a yellow block pairs the brand statement with a satisfaction guarantee and trusted-brands card.

06No minimum and client logos

One item or a thousand, and who already orders.

A short call to action states "no minimum order required", followed by a black strip of client logos from startups to global names.

07How ordering works

Custom merch in three steps.

Choose your product, upload your logo, get it fast. Large numbers and one supporting line each keep the process scannable.

08Categories

Illustrated mascots for every category.

Drinkware, clothing, office supplies and bags each get a nerdy illustrated character in a tall capsule card, alternating white and yellow so the row has rhythm.

09Best-sellers

Real products with real prices.

A dark section with category tabs shows best-selling items with starting prices and colour swatches, followed by three benefit pills: no minimum orders, quality guarantee and sustainability.

10About and languages

Local, multilingual and easy to reach.

Phone, email and address sit above a short company story, beside a dark card that speaks to customers in Portuguese, Luxembourgish and German, before a full footer with newsletter sign-up.

11Decisions

Key design decisions

Capsule shapes everywhere

Rounded capsules for the hero images, categories and buttons give the brand one friendly, recognisable shape.

Prices on the product cards

"From €6,70" on each card tells buyers this is a store, not a request-a-quote form.

Yellow and black only

A strict two-colour palette keeps a busy, product-heavy page bold and easy to scan.

12Design system

Visual language

Signal yellow and black on white, a geometric sans for headings and body, capsule-shaped cards and pill buttons, and line-art mascots for the categories.

Signal yellow#FFC400

13Outcome

What was delivered

Nerds gets a store homepage that sells custom merch like everyday products: clear promises, visible prices, proof from real clients and a playful identity that stands out from generic promo-product sites.

If the project continues, next I would:

  • Design the logo upload and live preview tool
  • Add a bulk-order price calculator
  • Create product pages for each category
