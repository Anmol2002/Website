The JournalPersonal journaling app · iOS

A quieter way to capture the day. - journaling iOS mobile app UI/UX case study

The Journal is a personal journal built for a daily writing habit. I designed the end-to-end mobile experience in Figma around one idea: writing something down should take seconds and finding it again should never depend on remembering what you called it.

Role
UI/UX Designer
Platform
Mobile app · iOS
Scope
UX flow, UI design, interaction, prototyping
Tool
Figma
01The tension02The core journey03Information architecture04Visual language05The experience

01The tension

One journal, two opposite moments.

Capture happens in seconds, mid-day, with nothing prepared. Recall happens later, with a rough sense of when - rarely of a title. Optimise only for writing and entries pile up unfindable; optimise only for organising and the blank page gets heavier.

The design question

How might one screen invite a new thought - and lead back to an old one?

One action to beginThe first screen asks a question and offers a single way to answer it.
Time as the indexDates, not folders or titles, are the main way back in.
Content gets the roomLists stay compact so the entry itself can be generous.

02The core journey

A loop, not a stack of screens.

Capture and recall share one path. Every screen either starts an entry or leads back to one.

  1. OpenA first-run screen with a single word on the button.
  2. PromptHome opens with a question; Add Journal is the only black element.
  3. WriteDate on top, photo first, words below, tools in thumb reach.
  4. Browse by dayToday is pre-selected, so the list opens on what was just written.
  5. Widen to weekOne toggle switches the scope; the range pill steps through time.
  6. RevisitAny card opens the full entry; + is one tap away everywhere.

03Information architecture

Then I made time the navigation layer.

Instead of parking history in a separate archive, I put the calendar on the home screen itself. People rarely remember what they titled an entry - they usually remember roughly when it happened.

By date · primary
The week strip lives on Home; Day/Week and the range pill take over in the list.
By word
Search sits directly under the prompt, with filters in the field.
By browsing
All Notes - the escape hatch when neither date nor word is known.
One entry, two densities
Rich where there is one thing to look at, compressed where there are many.

04Visual language

Soft surfaces, one point of contrast.

The palette comes straight from the splash gradient. Full-strength violet is reserved for actions and the home header; everything else sits on pale lavender so photos and words carry the colour.

Violet#9226FFActions, FAB, toggles
Periwinkle#A4B1FFGradient partner

Serif display · the human voice
Wordmark, the daily prompt and button labels.
Poppins · the content
Titles, body copy, dates and metadata.

05The experience

From a quick thought to a memory worth returning to.

Low-friction captureOne question, one primary button. No setup and no decision.
Recall by timeThe calendar lives on Home and in every list, so finding starts from when, not what.
Content-first readingThe entry gives the photo and words the screen; tools wait at the bottom.
One continuous loopA persistent + and a shared card language make capture and recall one place.

Write it in a second. Find it by the day it happened.
