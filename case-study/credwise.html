FINORAProduct design case study

Everyday finance, made clear enough to act on. - personal finance fintech mobile app UI/UX case study

A mobile personal-finance product that brings net worth, credit, spending, payments, cards and guidance into one connected experience.

Project
FINORA
Product
Personal finance and money management app
Platform
Mobile app
Scope
UX architecture, user flows, UI design, design system

01The product

One financial picture, not a pile of separate tools.

FINORA brings net worth, credit, spending, payments, cards and guidance together, so users can see where they stand and decide what to do next.

MoneyNet worth, balances, spending and savings.
CreditScore, its drivers and every account.
PaymentsUPI, bills, transfers and linked banks.
CardsBalances, limits, dues and rewards.
InsightsMonthly reviews, categories, savings rate.
GuidanceAn AI coach with plans and comparisons.

02The challenge

Financial information is everywhere. Knowing what to do with it is the hard part.

Too much competes for attentionBalances, scores, bills, offers and payments all ask to be seen first.
Health must read at a glanceUsers need a quick sense of where they stand before going deeper.
Numbers alone overwhelmA score or a total only helps when the reasons behind it are clear.
Decisions need confidenceCards and loans should be understood and compared before anything is committed.
The design question

How might one mobile app make money decisions feel clearer, not heavier?

03UX strategy

A simple loop moves users from information to action.

The product is organised around what users are trying to do, not around financial products. Each area answers one question, then points to the next.

  1. SeeWhere do I stand? Home dashboard
  2. UnderstandWhy and is it changing? Credit score, insights
  3. ActWhat can I do now? Payments, cards, offers
  4. TrackWhat just happened? Transactions, alerts
  5. ImproveWhat should I do next? AI financial coach

The loop closes on the home screen.

The coach’s latest suggestion reappears there as the user’s next move.

04Dashboard and information architecture

One home for the user’s financial life.

Users need to understand their position before deciding what to do. The home screen reads top-down: overall picture, then health, then obligations, then the next move.

Position · 1-2
Net worth as one anchor number, then quick actions: send money, pay bills, UPI and investments.
Health · 3-4
A financial health score out of 100, then a credit snapshot with change and utilization.
Habits and obligations · 5-7
Four months of spending side by side, savings progress and upcoming bills with autopay status.
Next move · 8-9
One concrete AI suggestion, then recent transactions as evidence for everything above.

05Credit and financial insights

Make financial health easier to understand.

Information becomes useful when users know what it means and what to do next. Credit and spending follow the same pattern: signal, explanation, action.

06Cards, loans and financial offers

Financial products, presented as choices, not adverts.

Offers sit next to the user’s real financial picture, so they must be quick to scan and easy to judge. Each leads with benefit, surfaces the deciding facts and asks for one action.

  1. Benefit firstThe name and a one-line promise lead. Detail comes after.
  2. Deciding facts up frontTwo highlighted benefits or rate and tenure for a loan.
  3. One clear actionA single full-width button leaves no doubt about the next step.
  4. Quiet statusA small label adds context without competing with the card art.
Products the user already ownsEvery card shares one object model: balance, status, controls, limits, dues and activity.
Comparison before commitmentBorrowing options show rate range and tenure side by side, leading to costs and eligibility first.

07Payments and transactions

Every payment stays easy to find.

Paying should take seconds and understanding it later should take no effort. The flow runs from one payments surface, through clear confirmation, into a searchable trail.

Pay in a few tapsScan QR leads. Send, request, transfer and balance checks follow.
Confirm with clear feedbackAmount, card and category, plus a standing reminder to check the recipient before entering a UPI PIN.
Track without effortSearch, filter and day-grouped history. Transfers stay visible but never inflate spending totals.

08AI financial coach

Turning financial information into a conversation about what’s next.

The coach doesn’t add new data. It reads what the product already knows and turns it into a few practical moves the user can act on or ask about.

ContextualSuggestions reference the user’s credit, savings, spending and dues, not generic tips.
ActionableEvery recommendation closes with one step: check autopay, review a goal, set a dining cap.
CautiousPlans and offers are framed as illustrative and the final decision stays with the user.

09Design system and full product

Different financial experiences, one consistent product language.

A small set of tokens and reusable components keeps credit, spending, cards, payments and guidance feeling like one product.

10Outcome

From financial information to a connected everyday experience.

One starting pointNet worth, health, credit, spending, savings and bills share a single home.
Numbers with meaningCredit and spending pair every signal with its drivers and a next step.
Choices, not advertsCards and loans show the deciding facts up front, with one clear action.
A traceable money trailPayments flow into a searchable, filterable history.
Guidance from real dataThe coach builds suggestions from the user’s own picture.
One product languageShared tokens and components hold the whole product together.

The goal was never to show more financial data. It was to make the next money decision easier.
