IMPACTMobile learning app · UI/UX case study

Small enough to start. Visible enough to continue. - mobile learning e-learning app UI/UX case study

IMPACT is a mobile learning app built around 10-15 minute sessions. I designed its flows, wireframes, interface and Figma prototype - shaping how a few minutes of learning connect to a course, a weekly goal and a streak.

Role
UI/UX Designer, mobile app designer
Platform
Mobile app
Scope
User flows, wireframes, UI design, prototype
Tool
Figma
01The design tension02Designing the learning loop03Making progress visible04A calm, action-led UI05The final experience

01The design tension

A session lasts ten minutes. The journey lasts weeks.

The interface needed to make a small learning action feel meaningful while still showing the larger journey. Every key screen answers two questions at once: what can I do right now - and where is this taking me?

10-15minutes per session
20lessons per course
150minute weekly goal
7day streak
  1. 10 minutesMicro-sessions on Home are sized to fit a gap in the day.
  2. One lessonLesson rows show their length and turn into an orange check when done.
  3. Visible progressBars and counters move on both Home and Achievement.
  4. Return tomorrowThe streak leaves the next empty day in plain sight.

02Designing the learning loop

A loop, not a menu - every screen points to the next one.

The home screen puts continuation first, discovery second and reflection last. From there, one path leads into the course, out to progress and back again.

  1. ContinueThe active course is the first card, with 3/20 and a one-tap arrow.
  2. DiscoverMicro-sessions are labelled with their length before the tap.
  3. LearnLessons, level, length and certificate sit above one fixed Continue Learning action.
  4. TrackMinutes, lessons and streak turn a finished session into numbers that move.
  5. ReturnFour filled days and an open Friday give a reason to come back.

03Making progress visible

One screen, five scales of progress.

Rather than a single completion number, the Achievement screen layers progress from this week’s streak to the whole learning journey. Each signal answers a different question.

Streak · consistency
“Did I show up?” Days are drawn as a week, not a number.
Minutes · time invested
“How much time have I invested?”
Lessons · completion
“How much have I finished?”
Weekly progress · short-term goal
“Am I on track?” 120 of 150 minutes reads as nearly there.
Learning progress · the journey
“How far have I come?” The broadest view sits last.

All five signals sit on one screen, ordered from most immediate to broadest.

So a single session always changes something near the top.

04A calm, action-led UI

Orange is reserved for the next step.

The palette stays small on purpose. Orange marks what to do, peach marks state and colour beyond that appears only where progress is being rewarded.

Orange#F37B32Primary action
Peach#FDDED2Status and tracks
White#FFFFFFContent space
Grey#9A9A9ASupporting info
Continue LearningThe only full-width orange block: one primary next step, fixed at the bottom.
Status and duration labelsState before the title and time before the tap.
Progress barThe same orange-on-peach bar at course and weekly scale.
Bottom navigationAchievement is a top-level tab; the active tab is the only orange icon.

05The final experience

From a ten-minute start to a reason to return.

Low-friction entryShort sessions and one clear next action lower the cost of starting.
Visible momentumStreak, minutes and lessons make every session leave a trace.
One connected loopHome, course and achievement hand off to each other as one journey.
Clear hierarchyAction, status and progress each keep one consistent visual role.

Small sessions. Visible progress. A reason to come back tomorrow.
