NailSkillzE-commerce + education

More than a shop - a digital home for nail professionals. - e-commerce and education website UI/UX case study

NailSkillz sells professional nail products and trains the people who use them. I designed the complete website experience in Figma - storefront, catalogue, product pages, courses, inspiration and partner pages - so a nail professional can shop, learn and get inspired without ever feeling they’ve left one brand for another.

Role
UI/UX Designer
Platform
Responsive web
Tool

Scope
UX/UI design, information architecture, responsive design, prototyping

01The challenge

One brand, three reasons to come back.

A single nail professional might restock UV gel, sign up for a gel polish training and browse seasonal nail art - sometimes in the same visit. Each of those needs a different kind of page, yet all of them had to feel like one brand.

ShopRestock quickly across a deep catalogue - gels, acrylics, liquids, care, tools and equipment.
LearnChoose a training at the right level, online or in person, in French or Dutch.
GrowFind ideas, see who created them and come back for what’s new.
The UX question

How might we connect shopping, learning and inspiration in one experience - without the interface feeling overwhelming?

  1. Where can I go?Training Courses, Community and Shop the Look sit in the top bar beside search, language and cart.
  2. What can I buy?Product categories stay one click away on every page.
  3. Why trust this shop?A thin bar carries 2,000+ salons, buy now pay later, 60-day returns and Google ratings.

02UX strategy

A connected journey, not a set of isolated pages.

I mapped the site as one loop rather than separate sections. Each stage hands the user to the next - and the loop can start anywhere, because a professional might arrive through a product, a course or a look.

  1. ArriveHomepage
  2. BrowseCollection
  3. DecideProduct
  4. LearnCourse
  5. ReturnInspiration

Bridges: no page is a dead end

Course → Shop“What products do you need?” lists the kit for each training as a discounted bundle.
Look → ShopEvery manicure shows the products used, each with its own Add to cart.
Product → Partner“More benefits as a nail salon” turns a shopper into a business account.

03Information architecture

Make a deep catalogue feel like a path, not a wall.

Ten product families, many brands, variants and offers. I didn’t want to hide any of it - I wanted to make it legible. Every step narrows the choice while keeping the next step visible.

  1. Category railHomepage tiles reappear as a rail, so users switch category sideways.
  2. Filters pros useIn stock, brand, colour, plus “Without HEMA” and “Solvent-free”.
  3. Savings at card levelOffer badges and struck-through prices before the click.
  4. Help when unsureA “Not sure?” card leads to a product finder.

04Product experience

The product page answers before the user asks.

A professional buying a base coat wants to know three things fast: is it right, is it in stock and when will it arrive? I put all three above the fold, then layered depth underneath.

VisualsLarge packshot first, then the product in use and short application videos.
Information hierarchyBrand, In Stock and SKU above the name; price with an “excl. VAT” pill.
Decision support60-day returns, free shipping threshold and same-day dispatch cut-off answered at the button.
Below the foldPros and cons, reviews and Q&A, similar products and recently viewed.

05Education

Shopping is only one part of the professional journey.

Training is where NailSkillz builds long-term relationships, so I didn’t design it as a separate microsite. Courses live in the main navigation, use the same components as the shop and point back to it when students need supplies.

Same anatomy, different content.

If you know how to buy a gel, you already know how to book a course. The course simply adds “Follow training offline”.

06Content and inspiration

The experience continues after checkout.

“Nagel inspiratie” gives professionals a reason to visit when they don’t need to restock. It reads like an editorial lookbook - but every look leads back to the products and the person who created it.

Content isn’t an extra page - it’s another way into the shop.

07Professional ecosystem

Every kind of professional gets a clear next step.

Salon owners want trade terms, experienced stylists want to teach or represent the brand and everyone sometimes needs a human. Each need gets its own entry point and a short, focused form.

SupportA contact form beside phone and email, with opening hours as scannable day cards.
PartnerBusiness customer, Ambassador, Instructor and Distributor, each with one “Contact us” action.
One formEvery route ends in one collaboration form with a partnership type field.

08Visual design system

Designed as a system, not as individual screens.

Every page is assembled from the same small set of parts. The palette stays black, blush and white so product photography always carries the colour.

Ink#000000Navigation, headlines
Blush#EEB9D6Primary buttons, hero
Blush tint#FDF8FBSection backgrounds
Neutral#F2F2F2Chips, inputs
Offer#FF6565Offer badges
White#FFFFFFCanvas, cards

09The final experience

From shopping for products to growing as a professional.

Clearer product discoveryCategories, filters and cards that narrow a deep catalogue step by step.
More confident purchasingStock, VAT, returns and dispatch answered at the button.
Connected learningCourses that look, behave and buy like products - and link back to them.
A reason to returnInspiration, creators and community beyond checkout.

Shop. Learn. Grow.
