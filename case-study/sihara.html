SiharaProduct design case study

Sihara: your personal AI retreat and workshop producer. - AI SaaS retreat planner UI/UX case study

An AI-powered retreat management SaaS designed to turn planning, budgeting, follow-ups and marketing into a calmer, more guided experience.

Role
UI/UX Designer
Focus
Dashboard design · UX strategy · design system
Platform
SaaS / web
Deliverables
UX/UI · wireframes · responsive design
01Design thesis02What problem does it solve?03How the experience works04Visual design05Building trust06What the design achieved

01Design thesis

Make a complex operational product feel like a calm conversation.

Retreat leaders juggle venues, dates, facilitators, transport, suppliers, budgets, documents, deadlines and marketing - usually on top of the creative work itself. Sihara had to reduce that cognitive load, not become one more system to manage.

GuideMake the next step clear.
StructureTurn complexity into manageable stages.
ReassureMake progress and answers visible.
Move forwardKeep users focused on the retreat.

02What problem does it solve?

One AI assistant. A whole retreat operation.

Sihara acts as a proactive retreat producer. Instead of asking organisers to learn a large dashboard, it turns a rough idea into a working plan, then keeps the money, paperwork and promotion moving alongside it.

  1. VisionTurn a rough idea into a structured retreat plan.
  2. PlanningOrganise venue, dates, transport, schedule and facilitators.
  3. ProfitKeep expenses and quotes connected to financial visibility.
  4. OperationsManage documents, suppliers, reminders and follow-ups.
  5. GrowthCreate landing pages, social posts and email sequences.

03How the experience works

From “I have an idea” to “I know what happens next.”

The core strategy reframes SaaS as a guided conversation. Organisers don’t navigate tabs - they talk to Sihara in a chat they already use and the plan, numbers and next steps come back to them.

Tell · your vision›Build · the plan›Track · budget + profit›Manage · docs + follow-ups›Fill · marketing

Three UX decisions behind the flow

ConversationalUsers describe what they need in plain language instead of learning where features live.
ProactiveThe product surfaces reminders, next steps and follow-ups before the user has to ask.
Visible valueEvery stage is framed around an outcome organisers care about: a plan, a profit figure, a full house.

04Visual design

Make the value obvious, then make the next action easy.

The visual language carries the same calm as the conversation and the pricing page is where that clarity has to turn into a decision.

Navy#07145FHierarchy, headings, structure
Soft pink#E7B1CFWarmth and brand personality
Green#4FA56AActions, progress, positive states
Warm white#FFFCFESpacious, calm surfaces

Pricing UX

Clear tiersPlans are told apart by user scale and use case, not a long feature matrix.
Visual hierarchyThe raised middle card creates one focal point without overwhelming the choice.
Action at the endEvery card ends with the same CTA, so the next step is always in the same place.
Every screen should answer

What is this? Why does it matter? What can I do next?

05Building trust

A good SaaS experience also designs for uncertainty.

Handing operations to an AI is a leap. The surfaces around the core flow exist to show proof, answer doubts and give partners a reason to spread the word.

  1. ExplainShow what Sihara does.
  2. ReassureAnswer the doubts early.
  3. ProveLet users make the case.
  4. Next stepOne clear action to take.

06What the design achieved

From scattered logistics to one calmer product story.

A human AI persona, a conversation-first workflow and conversion and trust surfaces that all tell the same story - so the product feels as simple as it promises to be.

Simplified workflowFive clear stages turn the product promise into an understandable journey.
Coherent visual systemNavy, soft pink, green, rounded surfaces and strong typography.
Product + marketingCore UX and conversion surfaces tell the same product story.
Scalable foundationReusable SaaS patterns for future product screens.
