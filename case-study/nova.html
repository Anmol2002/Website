NOVAE-commerce UI/UX design

A fashion storefront designed from first look to final order. - fashion e-commerce website UI/UX case study

NOVA is a fashion e-commerce concept for modern wardrobe essentials. I designed the full experience in Figma, from browsing and buying to accounts, service and editorial pages.

03Homepage

A storefront with an editorial rhythm.

Twelve sections alternate products and stories, so the long scroll feels curated rather than crowded.

  1. Photo plus storyA full-height photograph pairs with a short story for each edit.
  2. One next stepEvery edit ends in one clear next step.
  3. Varied scrollImage and text swap sides between edits to keep the scroll varied.

04Product discovery

Three routes to the right piece.

Shoppers arrive with different levels of intent, so the store offers browsing, a mega menu and search, each one short.

  1. Filters above the gridCategory tabs and filters for size, colour, price and material sit above the grid.
  2. Quick addQuick add on the product card shortens the path to the bag.
  3. Two more ways inA mega menu and search with recent and trending terms offer two more ways in.

05Product page

Imagery leads. Decisions follow.

A large gallery sits beside a compact purchase panel, so shoppers can judge the piece and act without scrolling.

  1. Feel the fabricA 2× detail inset shows fabric texture inside the main image.
  2. Choose, then addColour, size and quantity sit directly above Add to cart.
  3. No surprisesDelivery and returns appear before checkout; details fold into accordions. “Complete the look” extends the outfit below the fold.

06Shopping bag

Always clear what’s next.

Whether the shopper opens the full bag or the slide-out drawer, the total and the next step are always in view.

  1. Everything per itemEach item shows its variant, quantity and price, with Remove or Save for later.
  2. Total before checkoutThe summary lists delivery and taxes before the total and the checkout button.
  3. Stay on the pageA slide-out drawer confirms each addition without leaving the page.

07Checkout

A few details, then it’s done.

Checkout is one calm page with numbered sections, ending in a confirmation that tells the shopper exactly what happens next.

  1. Four labelled stepsAddress, Delivery, Payment, Review.
  2. Summary in viewThe order summary sits beside the form, with items and total in view.
  3. Two clear actionsConfirmation offers track the order or keep shopping.

08Responsive design

One product page, three screens.

The same type, palette and imagery carry across breakpoints. The layout reflows; the hierarchy holds.

1920 · Desktop
Gallery and purchase panel side by side.
768 · Tablet

390 · Mobile
The gallery stacks above product details.

09Tablet and mobile

The journey, in hand.

Homepage and bag adapt to fewer columns and full-width actions, keeping tap targets large and the next step obvious.

10Beyond the purchase

Built for the return visit.

Account, saved pieces and order tracking use the same calm structure as the shop, so after-sale screens feel just as considered.

Account dashboard
Orders, favourites, addresses and preferences in one overview.
Wishlist
Saved pieces with a direct Move to cart action.
Track order
A five-stage timeline shows exactly where an order is.

Also designed: login, sign up, order history, saved addresses, search with no results, empty wishlist, size guide, returns and exchanges, FAQ, contact, store locator, About, Sustainability, Careers and the journal with article pages.

11Design system

One reference, used everywhere.

Every screen draws on one set of tokens and components: buttons and their states, form validation, product cards, overlays and a single outline icon set.

12Outcome

Thoughtfully designed. Ready for every screen.

A complete fashion storefront, from first impression to order tracking, built on one consistent design language.
