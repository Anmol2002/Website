What Is a White-Label UI/UX Design Partner? A Practical Guide for Agencies

More projects without more hires. Here’s how white-label design partnerships work, and how to pick the right partner.

Quick answer

A white-label UI/UX design partner is a designer who works under your agency’s brand: you keep the client relationship, and they design inside your tools and process under an NDA. Agencies use them to take on more projects without hiring full-time staff, paying for overflow hours, a monthly retainer or a fixed per-project scope.

Agencies win more projects when they can say yes to more work. But hiring a senior designer for every busy month isn’t realistic. That’s where a white-label UI/UX design partner comes in: a designer who works under your brand, in your tools, while you keep the client relationship.

How white-label design works

You bring the client and the brief. Your partner designs the screens, flows and prototypes in your Figma. You review, present and deliver the work under your agency’s name. The client sees one team: yours.

Day to day, it feels like having an extra designer on your team. Your project manager shares the brief and deadlines, the partner designs in your workspace and your team reviews and presents. The client never needs to know there is a partner involved.

Why agencies use white-label designers

  • Take on more client work without permanent hires
  • Add skills your team doesn’t have, like SaaS or mobile UX
  • Handle busy months without burning out your team
  • Keep margins healthy with flexible costs

It is also a low-risk way to test new services. If clients keep asking for SaaS dashboards or app design, a partner lets you offer it straight away and decide later whether to hire for it full-time.

Confidentiality and branding

A good partner signs your NDA, puts your brand on every file and never contacts your clients unless you ask. If you want them on calls, they join as part of your team.

Working in your tools

The best partnerships feel like an extra team member. That means designing in your Figma, joining your Slack, Notion or Trello and following your file and naming conventions.

Before you start

Run a small first task. It shows you the partner’s quality, speed and communication before you commit to a bigger project.

  • Your Figma team and file structure
  • Your Slack or Teams channels for quick questions
  • Your Notion, Trello or Jira boards for tasks and deadlines
  • Your naming conventions and handoff checklist

Pricing models

  • Overflow support: ad-hoc tasks, paid by hours used
  • Monthly retainer: reserved hours and priority each month
  • Per project: a fixed scope for a one-off client project

How to choose a partner

  1. Look for case studies that match your clients’ industries
  2. Check verified reviews and response times
  3. Ask how they hand off files to developers
  4. Agree how feedback, revisions and deadlines work
  5. Start small, then scale

Communication is the deciding factor. A partner who replies quickly, flags risks early and asks good questions saves your project manager hours every week, and that time is part of the value.

What a first month usually looks like

  1. Week 1: intro call, NDA, access to tools and brand files
  2. Week 2: a small first task to set quality and pace
  3. Weeks 3–4: first client project, with regular reviews
  4. End of month: agree hours and process for the next month

After that, the partnership usually runs on autopilot: your team sends briefs, the partner designs and you deliver. The more context you share about your clients, the better and faster the work gets.

Is white-label right for your agency?

If your designers are fully booked or you’re turning down UI/UX projects, a white-label partner lets you grow without the hiring risk. I work with agencies and SaaS teams this way; you can read how on my white-label design partner page, or compare options in freelancer vs agency.

Quick answers

What does white-label UI/UX design mean?

It means a designer creates work that your agency delivers under its own brand. The client relationship stays with you and the designer stays behind the scenes.

Is white-label design confidential?

Yes. A professional partner signs your NDA, uses your branding on all files and doesn’t contact your clients unless you ask.
